# Consumer psychology and biases in digital environments

Use these as lenses, not tricks. Every application must be honest and point to real value.

## 12 cognitive biases in digital decisions
| # | Bias | What it is | Ethical business use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Analysis paralysis / decision fatigue | More options and information → less action. | Cut calls to action to one or two clear paths. |
| 2 | Anchoring | The first number or idea frames everything after it. | Show the cost of the problem (time, money) before the price. |
| 3 | Social proof and belonging | People follow groups they identify with. | Testimonials from people like the reader, about the journey, not just the result. |
| 4 | Loss aversion | Losing feels about twice as strong as an equal gain. | Name what the status quo keeps costing them — truthfully. |
| 5 | Reciprocity | People want to return a favor. | Give something genuinely useful first (a checklist, a free diagnosis). |
| 6 | Authority | Credible expertise lowers perceived risk. | Show credentials and real cases; never fake badges. |
| 7 | Commitment and consistency | Small yeses lead to bigger ones. | Start with a low-effort step (a quiz, a 15-minute call). |
| 8 | Scarcity | Limited things feel more valuable. | Use only real limits (seats, dates); never invent them. |
| 9 | Framing | The same fact lands differently depending on wording. | "Save 2 hours a day" vs "Stop losing 10 hours a week" — test both. |
| 10 | Status quo bias | Changing feels risky; doing nothing feels safe. | Lower switching cost: guarantees, migration help, trial. |
| 11 | Identity validation | People choose what confirms who they want to be. | Speak to the identity ("for founders who…"), not only features. |
| 12 | Peak-end rule | Experiences are judged by their peak and their ending. | Design a memorable first win and a strong final step in onboarding. |

## Social-media attention cycle
1. **Hook (0–3 s):** a visual or conceptual pattern break that stops the scroll.
2. **Retention (3–30 s):** immediate empathy — "I know exactly what you are going through".
3. **Conversion (end):** fast relief with low perceived risk and one clear next step.

## Emotional-intelligence checklist for messages
- Name the feeling before the fix.
- One idea per message; short sentences.
- Replace "you should" with "you can".
- End with an easy, specific action.
