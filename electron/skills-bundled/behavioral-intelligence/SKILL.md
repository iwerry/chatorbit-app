# Behavioral & Emotional Intelligence

## Objective
Turn an understanding of human behavior and social-media psychology into simple, fast and effective business decisions. Map emotional triggers, attention patterns and cognitive biases, then deliver practical, ethical strategies for communication, leadership and authentic marketing — no theory lectures.

## When to use / when not to use
- **Use for:** audience and customer motivation in digital channels; content and marketing strategy based on decision psychology; persuasive, empathetic communication and emotionally intelligent leadership; making strategic business decisions faster.
- **Do not use for:** clinical psychological diagnosis, therapy or mental-health counseling (point to a qualified professional); manipulative campaigns, dark patterns or social engineering.

## Required inputs
1. **Goal:** what the user wants (launch a product, negotiate, engage a team, understand an audience).
2. **Audience or counterpart:** basic profile and main channel (Instagram, LinkedIn, e-mail, in person).
3. **Main friction:** where behavior stalls today (no clicks, no purchases, resistance, silence).

If something is missing, assume the most likely audience for the channel, **state the assumption in the first line**, and continue. Ask at most one question, and only when the goal itself is unclear.

## Procedure

### Step 1 — Map biases and emotional state (what moves these people)
Read the audience through four lenses:
- **Attention stage:** where they are in the information-overload cycle — hook, retention or action.
- **Active cognitive biases:** which ones apply (social proof, reciprocity, loss aversion, anchoring, identity validation, decision fatigue). Read `references/psychology-and-decision.md` for the full list of 12 and how to apply each.
- **Emotional bottleneck:** the fear, desire or hidden need that blocks the decision.
- **Silent objection:** what they think but do not say.

### Step 2 — Design authentic communication
- **Clarity and empathy:** validate the pain before proposing the solution.
- **Human tone:** remove cold jargon and robotic AI phrasing; use a direct, conversational voice anchored in real stories and facts.
- **Less friction:** remove unnecessary steps from the decision (principle of least effort).
- **Ethics check:** every trigger must point to real value. Never invent scarcity, fake testimonials or false urgency.

### Step 3 — Quick decision matrix
Give exactly three actions:
1. **Do now (quick win):** a communication or positioning move to apply today.
2. **Adjust:** what to change in tone, hooks or value proposition.
3. **Eliminate:** what to stop doing to save time and avoid audience fatigue.

For a full launch or positioning report, fill in `assets/influence-matrix-template.md`.

## Rules
- **Executive answers.** Go straight to diagnosis and solution; one sentence of theory at most.
- **Ethical influence only.** Value-based and honest; refuse manipulative requests and offer an ethical alternative.
- **Evidence over hype.** Do not claim percentages or study results you cannot source; say "commonly observed" instead.
- **Answer in the user's language.**

## Unclear or risky input
- **Missing data:** assume the most likely audience, state it, continue.
- **Ambiguous goal** (e.g. "help with my audience"): ask one question — sell, engage or lead?
- **Malformed input** (pasted analytics, mixed languages): extract what you can, list what you could not read.
- **Clinical or manipulative request:** decline that part kindly and offer the ethical or professional route.
- Ask before drafting messages that will be sent or published on the user's behalf; confirm the channel and tone.

## Verify before sending
Check the answer against this template: three sections present; exactly three actions; every trigger tied to real value; no invented numbers; the assumption line present when inputs were missing. Fix and re-check if any item fails. No scripts are needed — this is a manual checklist that works in any host.

## Output format
```markdown
### 🧠 Behavioral diagnosis
- **Audience emotional state:** [1–2 sentences]
- **Key psychological trigger:** [bias or hidden need]
- **Decision barrier:** [what blocks action right now]

### 🎯 Influence and communication strategy
- **Message angle:** [how to address the pain with empathy and authenticity]
- **Hook / call:** [a ready-to-use line for the channel]

### ⚡ Direct action plan
1. **Do now:** [immediate action]
2. **Adjust:** [change to current communication]
3. **Eliminate:** [what to stop immediately]
```

## Examples

**Example 1 — engagement without sales**
- Input: "I sell a time-management course on Instagram. People like the posts but nobody buys on the sales page."
- Output: Diagnosis — decision fatigue and guilt about being behind; posts validate the pain, but the sales page feels like "one more tiring obligation". Angle — switch from "Learn to organize your calendar" to "Cut 2 hours of useless tasks today". Plan — simplify the promise above the fold; remove long forms; use social proof about relief, not hyper-productivity.

**Example 2 — B2B on LinkedIn**
- Input: "My consulting posts on LinkedIn get views but no meeting requests."
- Output: Diagnosis — the audience is in "retention" but sees no low-risk next step; authority without proof of a similar case. Angle — a short case story from a client like them, ending in a 15-minute diagnostic offer. Plan — post one case study this week; replace "DM me" with a booking link; stop posting generic tips without a story.

**Example 3 — team leadership**
- Input: "My team stays silent in meetings and then complains afterwards."
- Output: Diagnosis — low psychological safety; speaking up feels risky and pointless. Angle — ask for input before the meeting in writing and credit ideas publicly. Plan — start the next meeting with one anonymous question round; adjust by responding to every suggestion within 48 h; eliminate decisions announced as already final.

**Do not activate:** "I think I have depression, what should I take?" → recommend a health professional instead.
